# Design Principles

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Final  
**Platform:** Noory ages **3–8** · See also **`Animation Guidelines.md`**, **`Audio Design Guidelines.md`**

---

# Purpose

This document defines the guiding design principles for the AI Reading Buddy experience. These principles ensure that every design decision supports children's confidence, enjoyment, and learning while remaining aligned with Noory's brand.

---

# Principle 1 — Child First

Every design decision should prioritize the child's emotional and learning experience before technical or business considerations.

Questions to ask:
- Is this easy for a child to understand?
- Does it reduce stress?
- Does it encourage reading?

---

# Principle 2 — Reading Comes First

The story is always the main focus.

The interface should support reading—not compete with it.

Avoid unnecessary animations, notifications, or distractions.

---

# Principle 3 — Simplicity

Children should never feel overwhelmed.

Design should include:
- Few actions
- Clear buttons
- Large touch targets
- Simple navigation

---

# Principle 4 — Positive Reinforcement

Celebrate effort more than perfection.

Feedback should:
- Encourage
- Inspire
- Build confidence

Never shame or criticize.

---

# Principle 5 — Noor Is a Companion

Noor should guide, encourage, and celebrate.

Noor should never behave like:
- A strict teacher
- A grading system
- A chatbot

---

# Principle 6 — Arabic-First Experience

The MVP is designed specifically for Arabic-speaking children.

Requirements:
- Arabic interface
- RTL layout
- Arabic-friendly typography
- Arabic voice interactions

---

# Principle 7 — Accessibility

Design for as many children as possible.

Include:
- High contrast
- Large fonts
- Clear icons
- Readable spacing

Future improvements (specified):
- Age bands **3–8**: **`Child Accessibility.md`**
- Reduced motion: **`Animation Guidelines.md`**
- Dyslexia-friendly type (post-MVP)

---

# Principle 8 — Consistency

Use the same:
- Colors
- Icons
- Language style
- Interaction patterns

Consistency reduces cognitive load.

---

# Principle 9 — Emotional Safety

Children should always feel safe to continue reading.

If something goes wrong:
- Explain simply.
- Encourage.
- Offer the next step.

Never blame the child.

---

# Principle 10 — Design for Growth

The MVP should be simple today while allowing future expansion without redesigning the core experience.

---

# Design Checklist

Before approving any screen, verify:

- Supports reading
- Child-friendly
- Arabic RTL
- Accessible
- Minimal UI
- Positive messaging
- Consistent with Noor's personality

---

# PM Thinking

Good design is not measured by visual complexity. It is measured by how confidently and comfortably a child can complete the reading journey.

---

# Conclusion

These principles guide Product, UX, UI, Engineering, and QA teams to build a consistent, encouraging, and scalable reading experience for children.
